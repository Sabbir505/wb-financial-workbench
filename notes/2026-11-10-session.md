## 3. The guardrail method, in four steps

1. **Build the receipt list** (`guardrail.rs:98-123`) from four inputs, all delivered by the research service: computed metrics, announcement summary text, the evidence excerpt, and the brief's own CALC values (`workspace.rs:421-431`). Free-text numbers are tripled into 万/亿 variants.
2. **Find every number** in the model's narrative by regex, skipping dates, URLs, and identifier-bound numbers.
3. **Match** each against the receipt list at three scales, accepting if `|c−e| ≤ max(1%·|e|, 0.02)`.
4. **Unmatched → violation**, recorded as a byte span, painted red, listed with a Chinese hint.

Three weaknesses to state in the thesis:
- `_is_pct` ignored → the 0.02 absolute floor applies to every number, including 亿-scale amounts.
- 1% is wide at scale (1% of 24.83亿 is ±0.248亿), so planted near-misses must sit outside 1% to be catchable.
- Scale tripling widens the collision surface: an invented number can accidentally match a scaled variant of a real one.

## 4. The framing that got corrected

Three errors in the working mental model, now fixed:

1. **"We check whether the brief is correct."** No — we check whether each number appears in the evidence. Presence, not correctness. A brief can be flagged while being arithmetically right (the 1652万 case) and pass while being misleading (right number, wrong sentence).
2. **"We self-clean it before the human sees it."** No such step exists. Nothing rewrites a flagged brief. The only correction is a human editing in the detail view (approve-as-revised, v1 preserved, diff recorded). The rework cycle exists in the interface (`CANCELLED` → service resubmits) but happens outside the workbench and is currently unmeasured.
3. **"We measure what percentage of the time we detect right or wrong."** No single percentage exists. Four separate measurements at two units: guardrail recall and false positives (per number), reviewer detection with vs without the flag (per brief, needs humans), review time and correction accuracy (per task).

**Canonical one-paragraph version** (use as the core of Chapter 1):

> An LLM drafts a financial brief from a company announcement and computed metrics. Some figures in that draft cannot be traced to any source — the model calculated or invented them, and they are indistinguishable from the sourced ones on the page. A reviewer with a queue of briefs cannot check every number by hand. The workbench puts a deterministic numeric-traceability check between the draft and the reviewer: every figure that cannot be matched to the evidence is marked, and the mark travels with the brief into the human's decision. The human remains the only decider.

**The invariant:** the model never decides, the checker never decides, only the human decides. Nothing is gated — flagged and clean briefs both reach the reviewer.
